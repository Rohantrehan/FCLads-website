In the current patch cycle, opponents executing 71-depth tactical setups cause defensive midfields to collapse inward, creating artificial midfield dead-zones. If your first impulse is sprinting or attempting instant one-touch forward passing, you will consistently turn over possession in your own defensive third.

Beating this mechanic is not about reflexes—it is about mathematical spacing, baiting the AI press trigger, and immediately capitalizing on the vacated diagonal half-spaces behind their fullback.

01

Recognizing the 71-Depth Auto-Press Trigger

When an opponent plays with defensive depth set to 71 or higher, the game engine automatically flags your ball carrier as "vulnerable" the instant you take a heavy touch toward your own goal line. Two AI players step up synchronously while a human-controlled second-man press (R1/RB) pins the nearest central passing option.

1

Identify the trap zone before receiving the touch

Look at your radar before the ball reaches your midfielder. If their central midfielders are sprinting towards you in unison, the auto-overload is engaged.

2

Hold L1/LB to call the striker to drop deep

Calling your target striker backward forces their center back to either follow and open a gaping vertical corridor, or remain back and surrender a clean third-man bounce pass.

3

Execute the Driven Ground Pass (R1 + X / RB + A) into half-space

A normal short pass will be intercepted by the auto-tracking AI. The driven pass contains sufficient velocity to pierce through congested press channels.

tips_and_updates
Lads Pro Tip: The 180° Body Feint

Never sprint on your initial reception

When receiving with your back turned to the opponent's goal, releasing the sprint trigger lets your player shield dynamically. Tap L1 to face goal, let the presser commit his charging momentum over your shoulder, and roll into the open pocket using the left stick.
